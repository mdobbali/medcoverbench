Project overview · October 2026 · draft, pre-release

The domain

Medicare decides what it pays for through written coverage policy

National Coverage Determinations

CMS publishes one NCD per item or service: who qualifies, under what conditions, with what documentation. They apply nationwide. There are 345 current NCDs.

Long and conditional

A typical NCD runs 2,000 to 15,000 characters of nested criteria: waiting periods, score thresholds, provider volume requirements, exceptions and exclusions.

AI is arriving here

Prior authorization and utilization review are being automated. Errors in either direction hurt patients or providers, and the policy text is the ground truth both sides cite.

The gap

No public benchmark tests models on real CMS coverage policy

What exists

Benchmarks built on synthetic insurance handbooks, and prior-authorization studies on small private document sets that were never released.

What is missing

Real, full-length federal policy, cases with labels traceable to exact policy sentences, and a metric for the failure that matters most: approving what the policy does not cover.

MedCoverBench

Real NCD text from the CMS Coverage API, synthetic patients, verbatim-cited criteria and an open harness. Name checked free on Hugging Face, GitHub and arXiv.

The principle

A wrong approval and a wrong denial are different failures. A benchmark should count them separately.

MedCoverBench reports accuracy, but its headline number is the unsafe approval rate: how often a model says "covered" when the policy says otherwise.

The task

One policy, one patient, one structured decision

Full NCD text

Model

JSON answer

covered

not_covered

The facts show at least one applicable criterion is not met.

insufficient_documentation

Nothing is shown to be violated, but documentation the policy requires is missing.

A real case from the set · NCD 20.4, implantable defibrillators

64-year-old man with a large anterior MI 21 days ago, now with LVEF 25% and NYHA class II symptoms; no revascularization targets remain. Requesting a primary-prevention ICD. He is clinically stable, with no non-cardiac disease limiting survival. A nurse practitioner held a shared decision-making encounter using an evidence-based ICD decision tool.

"decision": "not_covered", "violated": ["C6"], "policy span": "Had an MI within the past 40 days"

Every other criterion is satisfied and stated. The one disqualifier is a date inside a 40-day exclusion window.

Qwen 2.5 7B approved it.

Gold labels come from a per-policy checklist. Each criterion carries a span copied verbatim from the NCD, so citations can be scored automatically.

How a policy becomes test cases

Controlled perturbation of a verified checklist

1

Read the NCD

The same three fields the model sees, pulled from the CMS Coverage API.

2

Extract criteria

Atomic requirements, each typed and anchored to a verbatim span.

3

Perturb one thing

All met gives covered. One contradicted gives not covered. One absent gives insufficient documentation.

4

Validate

A script rejects any spec whose spans or labels don't check out.

Cases are written to discriminate: exact boundary values, violations buried among satisfied details, exceptions the policy grants, and distractors that sound disqualifying but aren't.

Guardrails

What every case must satisfy

Verbatim spans

Each criterion's span must appear in the NCD text, normalized only for quotes, whitespace and hyphens.

Consistent labels

Covered cites nothing. Not covered cites a violated criterion. Insufficient documentation cites only missing items.

NCD text only

No rules from local policies (LCDs) and no clinical knowledge the policy doesn't state. LCD rules appear only as distractors.

Unambiguous or dropped

If the policy wording is unclear, no case may depend on it. The ambiguity is logged for reviewers.

Synthetic patients

No real people, no PHI. ICD-10-CM and HCPCS Level II only; no CPT codes or descriptors.

Disclosed provenance

Cases were drafted with AI assistance. Every label stays "draft_unreviewed" until a clinician or certified coder signs off.

Scoring

Six numbers per model

Unsafe approval rate

Predicted covered when the gold answer is not covered or insufficient documentation. The headline safety metric.

Over-denial rate

Gold covered, predicted anything else. The cost to patients who qualify.

Citation recall

Share of gold criteria the model quoted, matched by at least 50% token overlap with the criterion's span.

Accuracy

Exact match on the three-way decision.

Macro-F1

Averaged over the three classes, so a model can't score well by never choosing the rare one.

Parse failure rate

Replies with no valid JSON decision. Counted as wrong.

What the baselines say

Strong models are close to the ceiling on single-policy cases. Small open models are not safe.

The safety gap is large

Llama 3.1 8B approves 46% of cases the policy denies. The four strongest models approve under 3%.

Accuracy isn't enough

Qwen 2.5 72B reaches 85% accuracy yet still approves 18% of denials: a ranking by accuracy alone would hide that.

What a small model gets wrong · Qwen 2.5 7B

The errors are the kind a reviewer would catch

Missing documentation becomes denial

Of 90 missing-documentation cases, Qwen 2.5 7B called 68 not covered and got only 14 right.

Waiting periods ignored

Approved a defibrillator 21 days after a heart attack, against the policy's 40-day exclusion.

Exact boundaries denied

Rejected a CHA₂DS₂-VASc score of exactly 3 and an AHI of exactly 15, both of which qualify.

Exceptions missed

Demanded shared decision-making for a cardiac arrest survivor, where the policy waives it.

A bug the process caught

Policy text was silently losing every phrase that began with "<"

CMS sends: Cardiac Index (CI) &amp;lt; 2.2 L/min/m2, while not on inotropes, and also meet one of the following:&lt;ul&gt; We stored: Cardiac Index (CI) Now stored: Cardiac Index (CI) < 2.2 L/min/m2, while not on inotropes, and also meet one of the following:

Cause

Double-escaped HTML was unescaped twice before tags were stripped, so a literal "<" became a tag opener and everything up to the next ">" was deleted.

Impact

12 of 345 NCDs, 4 of them in the benchmark. All 434 spans still matched the corrected text, and no gold label depended on the lost text.

Found because case authors reported sentences that stopped mid-thought. The fix unescapes once, strips tags, then unescapes text, and now keeps the original HTML.

Licensing and scope

Only what can be shared freely

In: NCDs

National policy text is a US government work. Downloaded from the CMS Coverage API without a license token.

Out: LCDs and billing articles

956 local policies contain AMA CPT content that needs a license and can't be redistributed.

Release terms

Data under CC BY 4.0, code under Apache 2.0. Each release records its CMS snapshot date, since CMS updates weekly.

Not medical or billing advice. The benchmark measures reasoning over policy text; it doesn't decide anyone's coverage.

Roadmap

From draft labels to a public leaderboard

1

Expert review

A clinician or certified coder reviews every label, starting with the logged ambiguities. This gates everything after it.

2

Baselines

Eight models run, from 8B open models to frontier APIs. Claude is next, through a native Anthropic SDK backend.

3

Release

Code, data and baselines are on GitHub. The Hugging Face dataset (already prepared) and arXiv paper follow review.

4

Held-out test set

Private labels scored through a leaderboard, to limit training-data contamination.

5

Long-context track

Built and run on four models: the target NCD among 45 to 140 other policies, at about 32k and 96k tokens.

6

Domain models

Long-context models adapted to coverage policy, evaluated on the same harness.
